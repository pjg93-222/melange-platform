## Automatic identity reconciliation

The separate `run_identity_reconciliation_loop` function runs continuously in the sidecar. `IdentityWebIndex.enqueue_people` walks the identity/routing person inventory, grouping name variants for lookup and prioritizing possible duplicates; source-only catalog records do not automatically enter this resolver. A durable SQLite queue at `audits/staffer_live_runtime/identity_reference_v1.sqlite` retains progress across restarts. Likely duplicate name families are prioritized, and route endpoints receive a persistent priority boost. Changed source fingerprints trigger new checks; temporary failures retry with exponential backoff, and completed lookups are revisited after 30 days. Site access rules, request timeouts and download limits apply. Search results provide discovery links only; snippets never prove identity.

The evidence resolver automatically consolidates compatible names when verified records establish a shared unique identity, a distinctive covered-position history, or dated office/title overlap without an ambiguous cohort. A subject-specific official biography can bridge separate career histories. For other public biographies, two independently hosted references must each corroborate the same career transition; copies and common publishers count only once. Shared names or employers alone do not qualify. Contradictory names, suffixes, birth dates, saved separations and unsupported transitive matches block a merge. Missing evidence remains unresolved rather than being described as a confirmed duplicate.

Web requests and graph rebuilding run off-request. A replacement graph is built independently and swapped only after completion, so the last valid map, searches and paths remain available. Profile additions trigger another reconciliation pass. Every consolidated person retains source IDs, source histories and reference provenance. Automatic decisions are separate from explicit user confirmations and are reproducible from the raw records and reference cache. The current resolution receipt is `audits/staffer_live_runtime/identity_resolution_receipt.json`; the live page reports actual queue and consolidation progress. Canonical source artifacts and crawler checkpoints are unchanged.

## Remaining ambiguous identities

Identity review lists possible duplicates that the automatic resolver cannot yet establish. Normalized names retrieve candidates; professional history and source references provide evidence. Cross-source candidate counts depend on the current inventory, cached references and saved decisions. Kathleen's original match remains explicitly user-confirmed.

The person panel offers pairwise **Combine** and **Keep separate** actions. Combine requires an explicit confirmation for the selected pair and applies a consistent alias mapping to search, profile evidence and introduction routes. Keep separate saves the rejected pair and suppresses that pair from subsequent suggestions while leaving other plausible pairs available. Decisions are checked against existing confirmations to prevent alias cycles or contradictory separation rules. Explicit confirmation can join records from different sources, including Politico and payroll; matching names alone cannot.

`POST /api/staffer-identity` accepts `primary_id`, `alias_ids` and `decision` (`merge` or `keep_separate`). The service validates that the records are known people in the displayed review group. The review helper atomically writes the assertion file beside the **served** Python module, normally `<source-root>/fec/surfaces/staffer_user_assertions.json`. Rebuilding the dashboard overlay retains source records, dates, provenance and the original model weights. The page reports reindexing while the identity-dependent view is rebuilt. A saved decision is not a raw-source or canonical identity promotion.

Staffer (`objective=work_time`) uses a bidirectional Dijkstra search over payroll and directory work exposure. `work_sources=directory` is the explicit comparison mode; `payroll_and_directory` is the Staffer default. Detailed dated directory/payroll rows take precedence over trajectory summaries. Trajectories fill uncovered intervals. The index clips exposure to the routing as-of date, retains original future end bounds, and does not infer work before a supplied start. Admin employment is excluded and internship rows can contribute to observed headcount without creating work edges.

Near ties in Staffer routes. The cheapest route wins, and costs equal to 12 significant digits are a tie. A route ties for the cheapest when its exact cost, the real sum of its float hop and terminal costs with no rounding, is within one part in 10^12 (relative 1e-12) of the cheapest route's exact cost. Among tied routes the one with the fewest steps wins, then the one with the lower exact cost, and any remaining tie is broken the same way every time (the smallest person ids, read from the destination end). The reported cost is still the route's hop and terminal costs added as floats in order from the origin. Before this rule, which of two routes that cost the same on paper won depended on how the floating-point sums happened to round, and two searches could pick different routes. Alternative routes under `alternative_strategy=ranked` follow the same rule: the next alternative accepted is, among the pending candidates whose exact cost is within one part in 10^12 of the cheapest pending one, the one with the fewest steps, then the one with the lower exact cost, then the one whose person ids read from the destination end are smallest. Two alternatives inside that width can therefore be listed in an order that differs from their reported costs by rounding; alternatives further apart than that are ranked by cost as before. In the ranked list of alternatives, the tie window is judged within each alternative's own search, so two alternatives that tie only over the whole route can occasionally appear out of step order. `distinct` and `independent` choose each route with one such search.

Each shared-workplace exposure integrates the product of both people's concurrent-job allocations divided by observed peer headcount, with a workplace-type multiplier. Recognized shared-employee titles have factors 0.02 in the House, 0.05 in the Senate, and 0.02 when chamber is unknown; these are assumptions. Saturating strength and a 0.8 per-hop factor produce structural route costs. Uncertainty adds costs for unverified present access, age, and workplace concentration. Separate overlap runs contribute recency in proportion to their exposure; a brief return does not refresh a long old overlap. Source evidence and modeled-window labels are restricted to the actual joint intervals. Same-name records are not merged by routing. Member context affects office terminal anchors only, with a 15% maximum bonus; it does not rank person-to-person routes.

Ecosystem (`objective=evidence_cost`) uses separate association costs and bidirectional Dijkstra. Neither model estimates friendship or willingness. There is no wall-clock or expansion cutoff on route searches, although neighborhood browsing has paging and effort limits. Unknown people, insufficient work coverage, pending searches and exhausted connectivity remain distinct states. A Staffer search that finds no route answers `unreachable` with an `unreachable_reason` and a plain `message` that says why (an office whose current staff have no dated work at it, an origin or a destination with no usable workplace connection, or no path between them; the office reason is judged on the staff the search could use, and its message says "the staff this search could use" instead of "this office's current staff" when some were left out or excluded), an `explanation` that is that same text (an unreachable answer with no reason keeps the generic explanation), and, like `insufficient_work_coverage`, a `suggested_objective` of `evidence_cost`; the page shows the message with one Search Ecosystem button that runs the same search, from the same endpoints, under Ecosystem. An office search under Staffer leaves out the office's current staff who lack coverage (no payroll record and not an operational staffer) and searches to the rest, in every search it runs; every office answer (found, unreachable or `insufficient_work_coverage`) reports who was left out as `uncovered_staff_count` and `uncovered_staff_ids`, and which coverage they lack as `uncovered_basis` (`payroll_or_roster`, or `roster` when `work_sources` is `directory`: that comparison reads no payroll evidence, so payroll covers nobody there and a person with payroll records and no roster entry is left out of it), the page says so in one line, "N of this office's current staff lack the payroll or staffer-roster coverage this model requires and were left out" or, for `roster`, "N of this office's current staff lack the staffer-roster coverage this model requires and were left out" ("lacks ... and was" for one person; an answer with no `uncovered_basis` gets the first wording; a person with dated directory work can be one of them, so the line does not say they have no work records; there is no line in an `insufficient_work_coverage` answer whose origin is the person without coverage: no search ran, so the page does not say that staff were left out), and `insufficient_work_coverage` is answered only when the origin is not covered or none of the office's staff is.

## Performance

Verification and source indexing run in the background at service start; the page opens immediately and reports its loading stage. Name-prefix indexes, immutable graph revisions, bounded response caches, gzip and ETags keep subsequent searches and refreshes small. The browser caches names/layout and batches canvas drawing, coalesces repaints, cancels obsolete requests and skips unchanged graph revisions. Full person and edge provenance is fetched on selection rather than repeated in every map response. Source refreshes invalidate metadata and source-dependent caches.

Once the service is ready, and again whenever a new router is published (the day changes, the evidence changes, an identity rebuild lands), a background prewarm builds the Staffer work index for the page's default options and then the people index, one after the other, so the first route or page load after a publish does not wait for them. A request that arrives during a prewarm waits for that build (one build per work-index key, never two) and gets exactly the answer a lazy build gives. A prewarm whose router has been replaced stops before its next stage. It never holds the service lock while it builds. `GET /api/staffer-performance` reports its state and timings under `metrics.prewarm`: the status, the router it was for, each stage's milliseconds, the milliseconds of the one garbage collection that precedes its first build when a router was replaced since the last collection (`gc_ms`; a prewarm that follows no replacement, the first one after a start, collects nothing, because there is nothing to free and a collection holds every request thread while it runs, and has no `gc_ms`, nor has one that is abandoned before it builds) and the earlier prewarms. When a new router is published the service lets go of the router it replaced at once (its work indexes, hop-row caches and same-displayed-name index; the replaced router stores no work index or hop-row cache from then on, though a distinct pass or `avoid_people` request still running on it can store its same-displayed-name index, which is freed with the router; a request still running on it keeps what it holds, and a request that searches more than once, such as distinct or independent alternatives, keeps the one work index it took at its first pass for all its passes, so a publish between two of them costs no rebuild; a request that asks afterwards builds its own; no answer changes), and the collection that the next prewarm runs frees what is left of it before the new indexes are built, so the old router's indexes and the new router's are not meant to be resident together. That is not guaranteed: a request that was already running on the replaced router keeps its index until it ends, and no collection frees what it still refers to, so for as long as such a request runs the two can be resident together. Set `MELANGE_STAFFER_PREWARM=0` before starting the sidecar to turn the prewarm and its collection off; the release at a publish stays. A Staffer search skips two people who never stood in one workplace together before any pair arithmetic; `neighbor_checks` still counts every candidate considered and `pairs_scored` counts the pairs whose arithmetic ran. The people with payroll evidence are kept on the work index, and the same-displayed-name groups behind `avoid_people` and distinct alternatives come from a name index built once per nodes mapping (and dropped when the service installs a different mapping on the same router, so the replaced mapping can be freed).

The page reports service readiness separately from source exceptions and identity reconciliation; a completed crawl alone cannot establish final identity reconciliation. After the extraction task produces a new fully verified model generation, explicitly repin `GENERATION` and `VERIFICATION` in `staffer_graph_snapshot.py`, run the source integration tests, and restart this sidecar only. Existing Congress-layer graphs remain separate.

Name typeahead (`/api/staffer-search`, `/api/staffer-offices`) and the map projection read one published view (revision, static graph, profile people with their search index, identity aliases, router and office index) and take no service lock, so identity reconciliation, refreshes and route requests no longer queue them. A refresh builds the next view off to the side and publishes it with one assignment, so a reader takes one generation's references together and never pairs one revision with another generation's router or profile index. The view is not a frozen copy of everything behind it: successive views share the static graph and, when a refresh finds the same routing inputs, the same router. Such a refresh reassigns the router's node mapping under the service lock. Every refresh that rebuilds profile structures assigns each person node's `contacts`, outside the lock and before it publishes, on person nodes the previous view also reads, and one that rebuilds the router also adds user-statement hub nodes to the static graph. Each of those is one reference assignment, so a reader gets the old object or the new one, never a half-written one, but one answer can combine the view it took with the next generation's node mapping or contacts. A map snapshot (`/api/staffer-live`) takes its view and every live field it reports (poll metadata, source coverage, identity progress, inventory, sources, errors) in one short lock hold and then projects without the lock; a refresh that rebuilds the router publishes its view and the poll metadata in two successive lock holds, so a snapshot taken between them pairs the new view with the previous poll's metadata. The reconciliation loop reads the reference database before it takes the lock. The 30-second profile refresh keeps what it built when the accepted profile snapshot (content revision, prefix hash, receipt digest) and everything the structures were built from are unchanged; every integrity check on the receipt, the rows and the review assertions still runs on every cycle, and any change, or a forced refresh, rebuilds. A refresh that yields equal node contents leaves the route caches warm; they are emptied only when a label, kind or name status changes. Short typeahead queries use pre-tokenised words and pre-ranked prefix lists, tested to return exactly what the full sort returned.

Typeahead matches pieces of a query only against names, titles, aliases and office names; identifiers (node ids, politico:<uuid>, bare uuids, bioguide ids) match only when the whole query is the identifier, in any case: a whole person id or uuid puts that person first, and a whole bioguide id lists the staff attached to that member. The record browser's People found list and the map's query filter follow the same rule over the text a person reads (names, source summaries, sources and aliases in the browser; names and source summaries in the map filter) and over committee names. A committee id reads `container:office:<chamber>:<committee name>`: the committee name is searched like any other name (by word prefix in the browser, by substring in the map filter) and the part before it never is, so `agriculture` finds that committee's staff and `senate:` or `container` finds nothing by way of the id. A whole person id or uuid, or a whole member, committee or policy id, in any case, finds the people it names or is attached to, and the browser lists them first, with a person's own id ahead of the people attached to an id. The record browser keeps only each person's first member, committee and policy id, so a person's second committee or member id, or the name of their second committee, finds them in the map filter but not in the record browser.

Add `async=1` to either `/api/staffer-route` or `/api/staffer-office-route` to submit a background search. Poll `/api/staffer-route-result?id=<search_id>` for its result. `found` results carry `optimal=true`, `complete_search=true`, `steps`, `cost`, and oriented source evidence for every hop. Pending searches never become `unreachable`. Completed requests are cached by source revision, endpoints, date and options. Active jobs are not evicted by the completed-result cache limit. A search can be stopped, but identical requests share one search, so each page names itself. The page sends one random `holder` token per page load with every start (`holder=<token>`), every supersede and every cancel. `POST /api/staffer-route-cancel` with the JSON body `{"id": "<search_id>", "holder": "<token>"}` (same local-origin rules as identity decisions; `holder` is optional, 1 to 64 characters) lets that page go of the search and answers `cancelled` (no page holds it any more, so a queued or running search is stopped), `released` (another page still holds it, so it keeps running), `finished` (a finished result is kept) or `search_not_found`. A request with `supersedes=<search_id>` lets that page go of the earlier search as the new one is accepted, unless it is the same search. A `GET` carries none of the origin checks of the POST routes, so `supersedes` is honoured only when `Sec-Fetch-Site` is absent, `same-origin` or `none`; otherwise it is ignored and the new search still starts. A search stops only when its last holder lets go, so a cancel, a supersede or a closing tab in one page leaves a search another page is still waiting for running, and that page gets its result. A request that carries no token (an older page, a script) counts as one shared anonymous holder: such clients behave as before among themselves, so one tokenless cancel stops a search only tokenless requests asked for, and a tokenless cancel never stops a search another page holds under its own token. A queued or running search that nobody has asked about for 90 seconds is cancelled as well, whoever still holds it; closing a page lets go of its search at once, and the 90-second sweeper is only the fallback. A cancelled search reports status `cancelled`, is never reused, and the same request later starts a fresh search. Every pending response carries `routes_requested`. `partial_routes` appears only for a request of several routes under the `distinct` or `independent` strategy (a single-route search and a `ranked` search report progress counters only): each accepted route in rank order, shaped like a finished route. Route 1 is final as soon as it appears; the finished result replaces the partial routes.

## Route choices

The path finder defaults to up to three distinct introduction chains. Choose Best route only, Up to 3 routes, or Up to 5 routes, then select a result to update the map, contact, and hop evidence. Both Staffer and Ecosystem networks support person and office destinations. Office routes stop at the first eligible staff contact. Changing endpoints, network, count, or evidence revision clears the previous choices.

The route APIs accept `route_count` (integer 1–5; default 1 for API clients). Multiple-route responses retain the best route at the top level and add `routes`, `routes_requested`, and `routes_returned`. `alternative_strategy=distinct` greedily skips intermediaries from earlier choices, including same-displayed-name records for exclusion only. `independent` also skips earlier recorded workplace/organization contexts; missing context cannot prove independence. Each route is best under its accumulated exclusions; the portfolio is not globally optimized. Fewer results do not prove that no larger disjoint portfolio exists. `ranked` uses Yen loopless alternatives and may reuse intermediaries.

`avoid_people` is empty by default. Active exclusions are visible in the toolbar, form and result list, with per-person removal and clear-all controls. Saved links retain explicit exclusions and display them on restoration. Clear and Reset view remove them. Alternative-specific skips are named alongside their selected route; they do not become a permanent blacklist.

The current implementation is governed by `staffer_work_sources.py`, `staffer_closeness_routes.py`, `staffer_distinct_routes.py`, `staffer_routing.py`, and `fec/staffer_network/{closeness_math,relationship_uncertainty,member_context}.py`. The map and exclusion controls live in the adjacent JavaScript files. Static historical member rankings are a separate artifact and are not rebuilt by payroll promotion.
