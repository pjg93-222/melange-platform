# Mentat FEC accounting methodology

Version 5, September 12, 2026. Applies to every company, industry, cycle, collector and report. This is the required standard and a record of its current implementation limits, not a certification of historical totals.

Approved accounting direction (September 10): count original employee and corporate giving once, with PAC outgoing spending reported separately. The automated `tools/run_cross_reference.py` workflow now produces this provisional accounting view, its source comparison, supporting ledger and review queue before workbook builds. The console's **Cross-reference** tab lists each run's clear refunds, evidence-backed contradictions and unusually large counted receipts for inspection and publishes no totals, because the published workbook is the accounting of record; the full view is saved with the run. Magnitude is not contradictory evidence and never excludes a receipt. Existing workbooks retain the receipt view described below. The earlier isolated trial remains documented in [the test diff](../outputs/mentat-original-giving-test-diff-2026-09-10.md).

## The six-step process

The purpose is one coherent picture of the money: who supplied it, what happened to it, where it went, and which conclusions the evidence supports. All company, recipient, funding and partisan views must be traceable to the same accounting decisions. They are different views of the money and are not automatically additive.

| Step | Question | Required result |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Define** | Which companies, dates and kinds of giving are we measuring? | A dated scope covering the company roster, historical affiliations, FEC sources, reporting cutoff and accounting basis. Distinguish tracked companies from the entire industry. |
| **2. Collect** | Do we have the relevant FEC evidence, and what is missing? | Complete source responses preserved separately from interpretations, verified query coverage and an explicit list of missing or failed scopes. A successful search does not prove every relevant alias or disclosure is known. |
| **3. Identify** | Who supplied the money, who received it, and what does the transaction represent? | Evidence-backed identities, company relationships for the relevant date, and event classifications. Preserve competing matches; collection order must not decide attribution. |
| **4. Connect** | Which records describe the same gift or its later movement? | Links among original gifts, filing amendments, intermediary stages, asset conversions and refunds, with source evidence and dollar allocations. Unresolved links remain visible. |
| **5. Count** | What should count once, and in which view? | Original employee and supported corporate treasury giving counted once; gross gifts, linked adjustments and net giving distinguishable; PAC outgoing activity shown separately. Unknown ownership and recipient categories stay distinct. |
| **6. Verify and report** | Can we defend the number and explain every change? | Reconciliation in cents, independent accuracy checks, meaningful denominators, a dated report, and visible unresolved exposure and coverage limits. Comparisons to OpenSecrets must match scope and explain differences. |

For example: an employee gives $100 to a company PAC, which later gives $60 to a candidate. The original-giving view shows $100 of employee giving, and the PAC-outgoing view shows $60. They do not create $160 of original giving. Connected recipient information can explain where money went without creating another gift.

Changes to the system must identify which of these steps they affect. Preserve evidence once, reuse it across the steps, and recompute only affected decisions and reports. Every added, removed or reassigned amount must retain its reason and supporting records. A rule change does not justify recollecting unchanged evidence or silently rewriting the accepted baseline.

## What the accuracy standard means

The user clarified that “99.9%” was metaphorical: the requirement is a thorough, coherent and accurate system, not a literal numerical certification target. Demonstrate accuracy by checking decisions and coverage against evidence within a declared FEC scope and cutoff. Keep the following measures separate:

| Measure | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| Record accuracy | The proportion of independently reviewed records with correct identity, classification, amount and counting decisions. Review both included and excluded records. |
| Dollar accuracy | The dollar impact of wrong amounts, attribution, false inclusions, omissions and duplicate counting against independently adjudicated evidence. Specify the accounting unit and gross-dollar denominator before testing; measure absolute overstatements and understatements so offsetting mistakes cannot cancel. |
| Collection coverage | Which planned companies, aliases, periods, schedules and queries were completed. This measures execution of the declared scope; it does not prove that the roster or available disclosures capture all real-world giving. |
| Unresolved exposure | Undecided records and their gross positive and negative amounts, separated by cause: collection, ownership, event meaning or linkage. These are review exposures, not measured error rates; overlapping representations are not an error bound. |

Do not combine these into one reassuring percentage. Disclose coverage separately from interpretation and linkage. The audit must explain what was checked, its results and what remains unresolved; targeted large-dollar checks complement representative sampling. A review of collected records alone cannot measure unknown omitted records, so completeness needs separate source checks. Do not turn the user's emphasis on accuracy into an invented numerical requirement.

Reports should distinguish supported original-giving amounts, unresolved observed amounts and separately reported PAC activity. A verified subtotal is still a subtotal if scope or relationships are incomplete. The earlier reduction in missing form/line metadata does not by itself establish accurate attribution, unique gifts or complete coverage.

## Define the number before computing it

Every report must identify its companies and aliases, dates/cycles, collection cutoff, FEC schedules, funding sources, direction of money, amendment treatment, and treatment of refunds and intermediaries. An incomplete cycle is a dated snapshot. Comparisons must use the same scope or explain the scope difference in dollars where possible. Lobbying is outside this system's present task.

The current donor report measures **included itemized receipts at the named recipient**, with signed amounts and the established retention of original/amended rows and intermediary stages. It does not establish unique contributions across filings or end-to-end spending. A passing source/dollar guard verifies consistency with this accounting basis; it does not establish completeness of all political giving or correct every filer disclosure.

The original-giving view uses explicit filing-version and transaction relationships to select observed counted representations. Completeness of those relationships remains a separate requirement for a verified unique-contribution total. Preserve the historical receipt view alongside it and reconcile the difference. Do not silently change the existing view or relabel its sum as unique spending. A contribution and the sale of its donated asset are different events; the sale proceeds are not a second gift.

## Rules that apply everywhere

| Principle | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Preserve evidence | Retain original FEC rows, queries, cursors, filing identifiers, retrieval times and source links. Corrections append versioned decisions; metadata recovery uses a private copy and validates identity before updating it. |
| Prove collection coverage | Enumerate the expected company/alias/cycle work, record completed and incomplete units, and reconcile fetched IDs and pages. An empty completed result differs from an unrun or failed query. More rows or dollars do not prove completeness. |
| Preserve query meaning | Search every literal roster spelling; do not use company-name normalization to rewrite FEC queries or assume a shorter query covers a longer alias. A changed query contract cannot reuse an old checkpoint. |
| Validate a stable population | Require exact FEC result counts and unchanged counts within each paginated query. A final count matching any earlier count is not proof of a consistent crawl. Preserve failed pages and recollect the affected scope. Split a slow query into exhaustive date windows using the same terms; observed narrower spellings alone do not prove coverage. |
| Pin the intended source | Record the exact database and input/rule fingerprints. Compare against the accepted source by FEC ID and financial facts. Record additions, removals and changes separately, even when their net effect is zero. Reconcile genuine corrections without deleting the historical baseline. |
| Classify the transaction | Determine receipt role from the filing form, line and supporting transaction evidence. A company name, employee match, memo flag, large amount or intermediary is insufficient by itself. The same evidence standard applies to all industries. |
| Separate ownership and direction | Keep individual giving, corporate treasury giving and PAC/committee giving separate. Employee-to-company-PAC receipts and PAC-to-recipient spending are separate stages. A corporate-name search identifies candidates for attribution, not proof of treasury ownership. Preserve unknown attribution explicitly. |
| Deduplicate with evidence | Removing a representation as a duplicate requires a distinct retained counterpart, an explicit evidence rule, compatible donor/recipient/date/filing context and signed amount coverage. The receipt-view memo rule requires an explicit FEC back-reference and exactly one compatible parent in the same filing. Matching donor/date/amount or a shared report year/type alone cannot establish the link. Non-contribution exclusions use evidence of transaction role; they need not invent a duplicate parent. |
| Keep uncertainty visible | Preserve unresolved records with reasons and record/dollar coverage. They may appear in clearly provisional diagnostics, but cannot silently become zero or verified giving. A targeted recovery marked complete describes only its requested scope. |
| Reconcile every change | Account for additions as rigorously as reductions. Previous total plus signed record-level changes must equal the new total in cents. Separate source recovery, classification changes, exclusions and scope changes. Net-zero changes still require a ledger. |
| Preserve meaningful breakdowns | Funding and recipient tables are alternative views of the same receipts and must not be added together. Show the partisan denominator and classified coverage. D/(D+R) describes classified net receipts, not all giving or employee beliefs. If signed amounts make a percentage undefined or misleading, disclose that rather than forcing 0–100%. |
| Test decisions independently | Shared inclusion logic prevents report/QA drift, but agreement between two users of the same predicate is not independent validation. Use original-filing examples and synthetic counterexamples to test both false exclusions and false inclusions. |

FEC Schedule A includes contributions, transfers and other kinds of receipts, with different line meanings across forms. These distinctions are documented in the [FEC receipt data reference](https://www.fec.gov/campaign-finance-data/about-campaign-finance-data/about-receipts-data/). A conduit can produce additional disclosure entries for a contribution; see the [FEC conduit reporting example](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/filing-reports/contributions-received-through-conduits/). Receipt counts are therefore not a count of independent gifts.

**An “other receipts” line is not a blanket exclusion.** FEC instructions place genuine [hybrid PAC non-contribution account donations](https://www.fec.gov/updates/fec-statement-on-carey-v-fec/) and [national-party special-account contributions](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/filing-political-party-reports/additional-national-party-accounts-receipts/) on Form 3X, line 17. Recognize explicit account descriptions; otherwise retain the amount provisionally pending transaction evidence. Explicit interest, processing-fee refunds and asset-liquidation proceeds are different economic events. The generic FEC label mentioning interest/dividends does not prove that a particular receipt is interest. A promised refund is not an observed adjustment and does not justify setting a contribution to zero. These controls classify receipt meaning, not whether an intermediary representation is unique or an organization is the original treasury donor.

## Resolve the question the evidence can answer

Do not require whole-system completeness to classify an observed receipt. The cross-reference ledger records receipt verification and filing coverage separately. Clear receipt decisions are resolved automatically; missing filing histories and complete inventories become grouped collection tasks. Observed competing versions, contradictory facts, ambiguous memo parents and uncertain economic roles still require receipt review. A verified receipt is not a certification of unique dollars across every filing and route.

Use the strongest identity evidence before weaker heuristics. Full approved-alias equality, including normalized legal suffixes and equivalent spacing/punctuation, precedes prefix review in both scalar and batch employer matching. Prefix similarity alone does not establish identity. Historical individual-employer review holds are re-evaluated through that same matcher, with only exact approved matches released automatically. Preserve the original status, replacement reason and rule/input fingerprints; do not change stored FEC observations or reopen explicit source rejections.

Resolve economic meaning before requesting evidence that cannot change the decision. An explicit interest receipt or documented asset liquidation is non-giving even when its company attribution remains unverified. A special account designation does not transform explicitly reported interest into a contribution. Generic form captions and missing metadata are insufficient; conflicting transaction evidence stays unresolved. Classification as non-giving does not certify employer identity or corporate ownership.

Every new rule needs a positive example and a plausible counterexample, plus counts and signed dollar changes from a saved-data rerun. A smaller review queue is useful only when it comes from a documented automatic decision or an explicitly retained coverage task. Do not equate a relabeled coverage gap with newly recovered evidence.

## Efficient evidence recovery

Use saved FEC responses first, then reuse a filing for all relevant records it contains, and request only the remaining evidence. Where no filing-recovery path exists, use resumable exact-ID requests. Validate recovered financial identity; never overwrite changed financial facts as metadata.

Recover fields independently. A literal filing form and matching committee can be established even when an older format's amendment or coverage-date fields remain unsupported. This must not promote form-only evidence into proof that a transaction exists in the filing or is its current version. Cache the original bytes and interpreted fields separately; normalize control-field whitespace without changing substantive identifiers.

Checkpoint work and respect the shared request budget. Cache keys must include the source, requested scope, attribution inputs and counting-rule versions. Re-run publication checks on cache hits. Work can be prioritized by unresolved dollar magnitude, ambiguity and reusable filing coverage, but all unresolved units remain on the queue; prioritization is not permission to drop small receipts.

Run inexpensive structural and source checks before expensive enrichment or workbook generation. A classification change should reuse collected raw data. A source change should collect or recover the missing scope rather than automatically recollect every company. No performance optimization may weaken the evidence standard.

## Validation and release standard

Use anonymized, reusable cases rather than company-specific exceptions. Required examples include genuine in-kind gifts versus sale proceeds, contributions versus returns/interest, two genuine equal-amount gifts, linked versus unlinked memos, missing or conflicting filing context, signed adjustments, amendment chains, employee-to-PAC versus PAC-outgoing stages, missing source IDs offset by new IDs, and partial-cycle comparisons.

At minimum, verify that reordering input and replaying the same API page do not change the result; each included ID appears once in its receipt view; mutually exclusive groups reconcile to that view; identical inputs and rules reproduce identical decisions; every duplicate exclusion has a counted counterpart; and positive/negative changes reconcile independently. Claims about unique dollars additionally require validated amendment and cross-stage linkage. These are acceptance requirements; the implementation status below distinguishes what is already enforced.

OpenSecrets and other summaries are external comparisons, not target totals. Match cycle, cutoff, organizational boundaries and money stages before interpreting a difference. Investigate discrepancies against FEC records; do not tune Mentat to match an external number. FEC is the source of record for this workflow, while missing, late or inconsistent disclosures remain data limitations.
